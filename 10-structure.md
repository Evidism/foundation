# Structure

Evidism has no prophets and no clergy. Authority rests in evidence and in the review of peers. Every role below exists to facilitate, as set out in The Collective.

- **Members** are called Evidists. Anyone becomes an Evidist by choosing to, and stops being one the same way. No vow, fee, or record is required. Membership asks only acceptance of these ideals and open-mindedness toward the thoughts of others, inside and outside Evidism. No one is asked to renounce another tradition.
- **Stations** are local communities. A station forms when a handful of Evidists agree to gather.
- **Calibrators** are facilitators. Each station chooses its own, as many as it needs, and can change them at any time. They organize gatherings and lead rites. Where the law requires an officiant for a rite, a calibrator may serve, and this gives them no authority beyond the rite. They hold no special access to truth.
- **The Council of Review** is made up of calibrators from the stations. It keeps the record of this document and facilitates the review of proposed amendments, as set out in The Living Canon.
- **The Catalyst** is one Evidist appointed by the calibrators to represent Evidism. The Catalyst speaks for the Evidists, conveying what members have decided rather than a view of their own, and helps guide the Council of Review in its collaboration and processes. Like a catalyst in chemistry, they help the work along without deciding its outcome. The calibrators set the Catalyst's term and may reappoint or replace them.
- **Founders** are the Evidists responsible for founding Evidism. Together they convene the first station and the first Council of Review. Where the law requires named people to act for Evidism, such as to sign on its behalf, the Founders serve in that role until members name others. They sign only what members have decided, and founding gives them no other authority.

No member, calibrator, Catalyst, founder, or council is exempt from question. A claim stands on its evidence, whoever makes it.
